My Critique: In my original critique of the visualization, my biggest problem was how overwhelming it felt due to all the different elements and how I could not focus my attention in a way that allowed me to properly digest the information the visualization was trying to convey. Based on this, I wanted to make a physical sketch first of a general world map with a title and color scheme.In this sketch I simply redrew the original map outline and got rid of all of the numbers and other graphics. By doing this I thought that it would be less visually overwhelming and make the design more readable overall. I chose different variations of green in the color scheme simply because I had green colored pencils available. I did keep a gradient scale that told you the range of days each color was meant to convey. I did change the title to "Average Paid Vacation Days Per Country,” as it was shorter than the original title. 
Group Feedback: One of the biggest critiques of my sketch was that the colors in the scheme were too similar which I totally agreed with (student, MSPPM). One of my peers also gave me the feedback that because it was a physical map that it was harder to see the smaller countries (MISM, student). In my first draft I used different types of greens; one of the students who gave me feedback stated that green made would make the viewer think that the visual was about the environment (MISM, student). While I personally do not get that correlation when I was looking at it, I can see why they would think that and ended up changing it to a blue gradient in my final version. 
My Redesign:I do think creating the final visualization on Tableau was absolutely the right move. On Tableau, I was able to keep the simplicity I wanted while also allowing viewers to hover over each country and get even more information on the exact number of vacation days each country offered. I think it would make the visualization more engaging and interactive for viewers compared to the original design. By using a Tableau world map, it made it much easier to see each individual country while providing a clearer, gradient color scheme. Tableau made the information more depth while not being too complicated, which I believe gave the visualization the best informative and emotive qualities because of its interactive and straightforward design.
