Summary

For my project I will be analyzing how the popularity of songs with certain bpms (beats per minute) evolves per weather season. I personally listen to all lot of music all year round but I have noticed that I tend to prefer slower songs when the seasons get colder. When looking at music platforms like Spotify, I have seen so many different playlists (made by both the users or the platform itself) that uses weather as a themes. It made me think about the idea of "song of the summer" or even high energy albums like Charlie XCX's Brat, being called  "brat summer"  in 2024. Even well established music charts like the Billboard 100 now has a category of charts dedicated to "Songs of the Summer." I think it is pretty common to associate certain songs with specific seasons but I am curious to see what that actually means for audiences listening habits. 

Through this project I am trying to answer the following questions:
Is there a direct change in bpm depending on the time of year? If so, is it a gradual change?
Could the album release date have a greater influence than season? 
Could holidays like Christmas play into or disrupt possible trends involving bpm? 

The original data set I am using is from officialchart.com and focuses on the top 100 songs per week;  however, I will be expanding upon this data to better fit my purpose. For example, while the data I am using only gives the most popular song per week, I will be adding to the data by include the bpm of each song.  I will also only be using data from the years of 2024 and 2025.  For my visualization I will be highlighting the start and end of seasons, which will allow me to see to see if there is a change in the average bpm of songs depending on seasons.

Outline And Methodology

For readers, I want to make a visualization that is readable and simple so that they understand immediately what the I am trying to say. To do that I will be creating a line graph on Tableau that has each month of the year instead of weeks so that there is limited eye travel. By doing this, I do not have to number the weeks which will make the visual more relatable to my audience. Since there are so many songs per week, I will be averaging the bom of the top 20 songs each week. I will also be limiting the years to only 2024 and 2025 so that I can make a comparison between years while not overwhelming the reader with too much information.  I want the colors I use in my visualization to reflect the unconscious cues of seasons, with cooler tones for fall and winter and warmer ones for spring and summer. By using these types of colors I believe the seasons themselves will stand out without disrupting the data itself. Each color will change based on the start of each season. My title will be "The BPM of Popular Songs By Season" as it is tells the reader what is being conveyed while also being straight to the point.
